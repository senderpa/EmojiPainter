# Why I Make EmojiPainter

I am an independent artist. For years I studied programming concepts and learned to understand
code, but I struggled to turn that understanding into complete working software. Claude Code
changed what I could make: it helped me realize ideas I had carried for years. The artistic
decisions and direction remain mine, while AI-assisted development lets me work through the
technical distance between an idea and a working tool.

## Creative multiplication

I am interested in what happens when visual components meet randomness, combinations, procedural
systems, and a person's choices. A modest collection of parts can produce hundreds, thousands, or
potentially millions of distinct outcomes. The pleasure is in creating the system and then seeing
what people make with it.

An emoji stroke can carry more than color and shape. It can suggest meaning or symbolism, move
through time, animate, and have behavior of its own. EmojiPainter already explores emoji-based
painting, replay and animation, smudging, and several kinds of canvas. I want to keep exploring
custom strokes and more layered ways to make images.

## What exists today

EmojiPainter is a released iOS and macOS creative app, alongside a browser painting pilot and an
open-source codebase. The app supports eight languages: English, German, Russian, Chinese, Korean,
Japanese, French, and Italian. I want its visual language to invite people in across cultures and
levels of technical experience.

## What I hope to build

I want to make open, community-oriented creative tools that help people meet through making things,
without requiring a traditional social-media platform. I hope to connect painting, animation,
custom brush behavior, and shared creative systems into new possibilities. My ambition is not only
to solve isolated software problems; it is to make room for forms of creative interaction that do
not exist yet.
