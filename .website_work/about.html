What EmojiPainter Wants To Be

EmojiPainter should work for children. Think: a very easy version of Photoshop. You open it and you can just draw with emojis. You do not need to understand complex menus. You click a symbol to import a image or play the timelapse, or you stamp it on to the canvas.

It should also work like a social creative safe space. A shared creative studio. A place where you can paint and publish directly. a community-linked space with no 3. parties involved !

Emojis enable simplicity. You can express a full idea with one icon. But you can also build difficult collages: layered.

The important part is global readability. Emojis carry worldwide understanding. The language travels. So painting includes a new shared visual vocabulary.

🛡️ Anti-AI Image Making — The Philosophy

EmojiPainter is built as a counter-position to AI image generation. Every painting here is made by a human hand, stroke by stroke, under a ticking sprint timer — there is no prompt box, no model, no "generate" button. What you see is what somebody painted.

Because the brush is the emoji itself, each artwork is a kind of encoding. Emojis are symbols with worldwide meaning: a single icon carries emotion, history and context in every language. A finished painting is therefore loaded with information — it can be read and translated by any human anywhere, but it cannot be meaningfully scraped, averaged or replicated by an image model. The meaning lives in the deliberate choice of hundreds of symbols, not in the pixels.

You can think of it as an encrypted way to paint: humanized digital images, readable by people, opaque to machines. AI can imitate the look of a picture — it cannot fake the chain of human decisions that emoji painting demands, and it cannot play the guessing game that grows around it.

This project comes from the same fight as hittheai.com and sprayar.com — tools and games by the same creator that put humans, streets and play before generated content.

🎲 Daily Topics & Mystery Topics

Daily topics: every day the app deals three fresh topics — the same three for every painter worldwide. Pick one, set a short sprint timer, paint it in minutes and upload it to the live gallery. The speed is the style: honest, fast, unpolished human work.

Mystery topics: the secret fourth challenge. You receive a hidden topic from a curated pool of 999+ topics (plus community suggestions that players vote on). You paint it so clearly that strangers can guess it — but in the gallery the topic stays masked behind hidden letters. Other players guess letter by letter; when somebody solves it, the painting is revealed and the solver is counted. It turns every painting into a little proof of human communication: can another person decode what you encoded in emojis?

Roadmap

Right now this is a free test run. A proof-of-concept state. You are basically inside an experiment. The current focus is:

  • Realtime multi-painter space. Multiple people painting live together.
  • Full scale animator features like on macOS only. Timeline, motion, GIF, MP4.
  • Augmented reality painter with AR image options.
  • Better long timelapse exports. At the moment most is still proof of concept.
  • Working full stack website with webshop and a gated user area for self moderation.
  • One-time purchase model. After the first 1k users the plan is to sell the application for 9,99.

Goal is stability and low friction. No algorithmic feed deciding what you see. You load art. You paint. You publish.

Inspiration

This can also be understanded as an homage to DailySpitPaint Group on Facebook.

I really liked the whole idea and I could really feel that community moment. As a huge fan and participant of the challenges I wanted to create a artist first save space and game-ify it even more. The 3 daily prompt. The leaderboard. The public gallery. The replay at the exact time of day.

So EmojiPainter is a kind of continuation. Same spirit. New tool. I hope John Conner maybe reads this at some point. Or you.
